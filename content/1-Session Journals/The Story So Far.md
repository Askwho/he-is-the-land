## I. Five strangers and a staircase

It began on a warm summer evening, with five people on five small errands who had never met: a tinkerer off to fix a clock, a rich family's black-sheep priest sent to find a blacksmith, a cobbler-gnome mending boots for coppers, a drunken young paladin bound for his wine bar, and a farmer two weeks into his knighthood carrying a message. The mist took each of them alone — an unnatural cold, a family keepsake suddenly *alive* and pulling like a magnet — and set them all down at the foot of a flight of stairs that had no business existing, before a tall narrow house with its lights burning.

The door opened itself. The fire died. A verse bled onto the wall about a beast below that wakes at midnight, and the grandfather clock struck six. The house lied to them — rotten feasts dressed as banquets, prop-book libraries — and, more unsettling, the house *armed* them: a shield, a longsword, crossbows, silvered bolts. In a hidden study they found a will naming two children, and a cruel letter that spoke of a place called **Barovia**, signed *"In darkness I endure."* Under a harpsichord they found a starving dog named **Lancelot**, and fed him, and that at least was simple. ## II. The children in the attic

The house fought and haunted by turns: an empty suit of armour threw Sirius over a banister and was dismantled by grease, holy force, and two natural twenties; a maid stepped out of a mirror not knowing she was dead; a shrouded crib held only centuries-old swaddling. Behind an attic door locked with an iron key waited two small skeletons in two small beds — **Rose and Thorn Durst**, who knew exactly what they were. *"We were locked in here. It got very hungry. And then we were no longer hungry."* Their mother had brought a cult into the house; their baby half-brother had been carried down to the basement for something about *innocence* and *sacrifice*.

Rose asked one thing: *"Bury our bones. Set us free."* Three bundles went onto Drew's belt — the children and, later, the maid. The children's dollhouse gave up the house's secret: a hidden spiral stair running top to bottom. Down it, in the dark, something was crying like a baby. ([[Session_002_Player_Recap|S2 recap]] · [[Session_002_Player_Notes|notes]] · [[Episode 2 — The House Remembers|listen]])

## III. One must die — the house's price

The crypts below held empty coffins for the whole family. Gustav Durst hung from the rafters of an underground bedroom; a black-hearted dead woman burst from the wall mid-discovery and went to dust with a firebolt through the heart. Deeper, past a painted statue of a gaunt pale man with his hand on a wolf's head, past shackled skeletons and ghouls that broke before Fëanor's raised symbol, a chant rose out of the dark: ***"He is ancient. He is the land."***

At the bottom: an altar over black water, a bundle of tiny bones upon it, and thirteen faceless apparitions chanting **"One must die."** Any living thing would do. Lancelot was standing right there. The party said **no** — Osric's sword came down on the altar itself — and when midnight raised a mound of corpse-flesh from the refuse, they hacked through to the glittering crystal heart at its core and shattered it. The mists parted. Outside, the freed spirits of Rose, Thorn, and the maid said thank you, warned that *a greater evil stays here*, and gave the devil his name at last: **Strahd**. Behind the party, the house was simply gone. ([[Session_003_Player_Recap|S3 recap]] · [[Session_003_Player_Notes|notes]] · [[Episode 3 — The Devil's Name|listen]])

## IV. The crown jewel of the Strahd empire

The road led to a stone gate flanked by headless statues — *"Welcome to Barovia, the crown jewel in the Strahd empire"* — crowned with the very sigil on Osric's family banner. Below it, a sorrowful village where curtains twitch and the first friendly voice belonged to an old woman selling suspiciously wonderful pies. At the silent tavern, **Ismark** — burgomaster for exactly one day — laid it out plainly: Strahd is a *real* vampire; the mists are the bars of the cell; there is no leaving. His father was dead because Strahd wanted his sister **Ireena** and the old man refused. Two favours asked: bury the father, and take Ireena west to walled Vallaki.

They buried the burgomaster with their own hands. And then Father **Donavich** begged them to help his son — swearing them, over and over, not to kill him — and led them down beneath the church to **Doru**: eighteen years old, clinging to the ceiling, undead, and begging in a boy's voice for blood. A crowning holy smite barely staggered him. The session ended there — the boy pinned and pleading, his father watching from the trapdoor, and no good way left to turn. ([[Session_004_Player_Recap|S4 recap]] · [[Session_004_Player_Notes|notes]] · [[Episode 4 — Mist Walkers|listen]])

## V. The rightful lord

It ended with a kindness. Lily bound Doru gently; the party brought his father down to hear the truth from his son's own mouth — Strahd had drunk from him, made him drink in return, and every command since sat on his mind like *a fetter*. Donavich turned away: **"Do what you must."** Doru wanted to die and had been forbidden to allow it; they ended it as quickly as mercy allowed, and the boy crumbled to dust — no rot, no body, nothing natural. Outside, Ismark confessed the crypt had been a **test** of whether they could protect Ireena, and handed over a hand-drawn map. *"You will learn not to trust people in Barovia."*

That night came a knock. What followed, the party mostly knows from Fëanor, because everyone else spent it charmed to the marrow: a visitor whom every one of them simply *knew* to be a friend. **Strahd.** *"I am this land, and this land is me."* He required a token of fealty from his hosts' guests, drank — not deeply — from a smiling, willing Sirius, and left them with a courtesy: *"I own this place. You may have your fun — but I get what I want."*

Morning brought the horror home, and the road west with it: ghost-lights crying *help me* from the trees; a standing stone ringed with the exact symbol on Fëanor's heirloom; seven wolves and two dire wolves meeting **CLOVER**, the small cheerful flamethrower Drew had been dreaming of for days. By evening — firelight, fiddles, and Vistani hospitality bought with the true story of Sirius's misspent youth. For one hour, Barovia was kind.

Then a large man stepped out of the great tent and looked at each of them in turn: **"What took you, Drusilla, Fëanor, Lili-grai, Osric, Sirius? You are expected."** ([[Session_005_Player_Recap|S5 recap]] · [[Session_005_Player_Notes|notes]] · [[Episode 5 — Friend-Shaped|listen]])

## VI. The threads of fate

Inside the great tent, behind two decks of cards on black velvet, sat a woman who needed no introductions because she already had everyone's: **Madam Eva.** *"It took you all long enough."* The reading was given freely — no silver crossed any palm — and the cards said what she would not. Five fates: knowledge of the enemy at **high water aglow with the light of the sun, hidden behind the walls of man**; protection in the **house of a dragon**, where a heart once pure has rotted into hatred; power at the **shrine of murdered gods**, beginning in an **amber palace of darkness**; an ally in a **hunter of hunters**; and the devil himself waiting at the end — **in the tomb of the only man he ever envied.** Then she read them each alone, and the cards knew things they should not: Osric's blood *marched beneath that banner* and must kneel and answer for it; Fëanor's stone *remembers a fire he has never felt* (his answer — *"Who told you?"* — is in the minutes); Drew's amber holds a question her blood has carried for generations; and Lily heard the thing everyone had been watching named to her face: *something sweet has taken root in you, little one. **Starve it. It is not yours.*** Only Sirius she turned away — *"your fate has already been read"* — and the card she pressed into his hand was **blank**, at least to everyone else. Outside the tent he told the others of a **lady of ravens** only he could see on it, familiar though he couldn't say why, who spoke to him once: *what was stolen is not fully owned, and the land can be reclaimed.* Her ravens, he thinks, are their friends — and his grandfather's raven feather suddenly sat very heavy on his lapel.

The Vistani fed them, sang to them, and told them careless truths: they pass the mists by an ancient bargain with the devil; the one passenger they ever carried through the fog never came out the other side; and Strahd has been exactly what he is for **seven hundred years** — if any living soul knows whom he envied, it is written in a book somewhere, because it is not in any memory. In the morning there was real bacon from beyond the mists, and there was Lily, elbow-deep in Osric's pack, fighting like a raccoon for a pie. Fëanor's patient magic finally showed the truth: **enchantment wound thick through every pastry — and a fainter web of the same magic around Lily's head.** She could not even promise to stop, and said so, which was the most honest thing anyone said all day. And since the reading, something has settled on all five of them, woven tight and drawn taut. The land has noticed. **They are stronger than they were.**

The road west gave them a rider — **Aragal** of the Vistani, bound for the camp outside Vallaki, where his people are not welcome — who warned of wolves and crying lights and asked one kindness: a toy from **Blinsky's** shop for his daughter **Araba**, and twenty gold for the trouble. And at dusk, with Ireena sensibly insisting on shelter, the road gave them a **windmill**, leaning on its rise with a henge of old standing stones crouched behind it and the smell of fresh pies rolling off it, warm and sweet and wrong. Granny came up the road with her little cart before the scouting was half done. *They are enchanting pies, I will admit — it's good for business.* Then everyone heard what was making the muffled sounds inside the cart, and it was not pies.

She ran — impossibly, horribly fast — shrieking two names at the mill: *Bella! Ophelia!* In the cart: a woman bound in a **silver net**, begging *not* to be freed — there is a full moon above the clouds, and the silver is all that keeps her human; the hags already took her partner inside. The mill door burst open on three of them, one wearing Granny's face gone warped and purple — **Morgantha** — *"You shouldn't have poked your nose in, dearies."* Now Osric holds the doorway with claw-marks in his armour, Lily's daggers have been answered with a warning about *their best customer*, and Sirius — wine-soft, sun-loving Sirius — stands in the gloom with **black raven wings spread from his back and feathers dripping from his eyes like tears.** Nobody is running. ([[Session_006_Player_Recap|S6 recap]] · [[Session_006_Player_Notes|notes]])

## VII. All will be well

They did not win at the windmill. Bella looked at the winged terror in the doorway — *"you, winged one"* — and Sirius was a **frog**. Morgantha frogged Osric a breath later, and into the middle of three cackling horrors ran **Lily**, claws opening her back, to scoop up both frogs and sprint for the road. Morgantha crushed **Clover** to scrap almost as an afterthought — *and take your toy, too* — and the three of them stood in their doorway and **laughed** the party down the hill, dragging the netted woman behind them. The hags didn't even chase. They went back inside, to the one still chained beside the millstone.

Her name is **Vasha** — bitten, not born; a Vallaki wine-carter's daughter who never came home. Her mate **Radu**, born to the curse, is still in that mill. And at the fire she told them the thing they will not unlearn: werewolves regrow any wound not made with silver or magic. The hags hack off their arms. The arms come back. **The pies are the arms.** There were never any missing children; there didn't need to be. They burned every pie they carried and called it a cremation, and it was. Vasha stayed to watch the mill; they gave her their word — *we come back for Radu, and we finish the hags* — and Fëanor's lore gave the word teeth: hags draw their power from the coven of three. Break one off, and the rest weaken. The hook is still in Lily: tied to a tree each night at her own asking (Sirius tied beside her, dagger in hand), one terrible hour each dawn, a little weaker every day. She is thirty-seven, riding at the end of a rope like a child on reins, and bearing it better than any of them would.

Then the road produced a **dire wolf standing in daylight with roses in its mouth**, and a letter on fine card stock addressed to Ireena. *My love, why do you tease me so?* He calls the party *ruffians*. And the line that matters: **speak my name thrice, and I shall be with you in the moment — to whisk you to our castle, Ravenloft. Your destined future lover, Strahd von Zarovich.** The party immediately began engineering summon-traps; Ireena, patience of a saint, asked to be made safe *before* being made bait; and Fëanor offered the only comfort available — he is still in the *gift-sending* phase. That night the raven came back — the same tufty raven from the village, days and miles behind — and when Lily reached out with her gift for talking to beasts, nothing answered. Whatever wears those feathers is **not an animal**. At a zombie-haunted farmstead the next noon, Sirius's black wings froze the dead where they shuffled — and **Ireena saw the wings.** She is on her knees now: an *angel of the Morninglord* walks with them, and they cannot fail. Sirius, who cannot imagine why anyone would think such a thing, knelt down and prayed with her.

And then: **Vallaki.** Real walls, a gate with forms — *are you now, or have you ever been, an agent of the devil?* — every weapon registered down to a broom handle, the devil's name a crime, a guard on every corner answering to a serial number, and three citizens in the stocks (one missed a festival while his mother died). The **Festival of the Blazing Sun** is in seven days and attendance is *punishable-by-punishment* mandatory. Say it with a smile: **all will be well.** At the church of St. Andral, Father Lucian took five strangers into his confidence before the soup came: the hidden crypt beneath the altar is **empty** — the bones whose holiness kept evil off this ground for centuries were **stolen six days ago**, right after the groundskeeper **Milivoj** came asking little Yeska about church secrets. The sanctuary they promised Ireena is just a building. She doesn't know. Tomorrow they find Milivoj at the orphanage he keeps.

The day ended at the **Blue Water Inn** — wolf steak, beet soup, the Martikov boys underfoot, a musician dressed as loudly *not-Vistani* as cloth allows. And at a corner table, an old, old man staring at the five of them, white as a sheet. His name is **Roger**. He is what remains of the *previous* set of Mistwalkers — the ones who came through the mists a lifetime ago, in what everyone still just calls the bad times. There were others once. There is only him. ([[Session_007_Player_Recap|S7 recap]] · [[Session_007_Player_Notes|notes]])
